1b · ESP32-S3 ↔ PCM5102 pin wiring

Exact pin-to-pin connections. Two independent I2S peripherals: I2S0 (blue) drives the LOW DAC, I2S1 (red) the HIGH DAC. No MCLK wire — each DAC's SCK is grounded so it runs on its internal PLL. GPIOs are the plan; verify against your specific board's silkscreen.

2 · Ground path — star topology

Every board's ground returns on its own dedicated wire to a single physical point: the PSU negative terminal. Nothing is daisy-chained. This is the whole reason the band-split helps — if grounds were chained, the class-D switching return current would ride through the DAC's analog reference anyway.

3 · Analog signal ground (rides with the audio)

Separate from the power-star above: each PCM5102's analog ground travels alongside its OUTL/OUTR wires to the amp input's signal ground, ideally twisted-pair per channel. This signal reference and the power-ground star meet only once, at the star point — so switching-current loops and audio-reference loops never overlap.

  • Twisted pair: OUTL + AGND and OUTR + AGND from each DAC to its two amps.
  • Keep I2S clock/data (BCK/WS/DOUT) runs short and away from the 24V amp wiring.
  • Physically separate the high-current 24V harness from all analog/I2S wiring — cross at right angles if they must cross.

4 · Physical layout & mute transistors

As-built floorplan: DACs + ESP32 across the top, the 4 mute transistors (T1–T4) in a row below, and the power distribution on an elevated tier above (its own board, standoffs over this one — keeps the 24V high-current wiring physically off the signal layer). Each amp's 2-pin "mute" jumper header is emulated by one NPN: a shared GPIO line mutes all four.

Mute bus (GPIO10) 24V (from power tier) 5V to amp mute header

Fail-safe: wire so GPIO10 floating/low at boot = muted; firmware un-mutes only after I2S is streaming and the PCM5102 XSMT is high. On shutdown, mute (GPIO first) before stopping I2S. GPIO10 is a placeholder — pick any free pin not in the I2S set (GPIO4–9).

Power budget reminder

150W ÷ 24V = 6.25A continuous. 4 amps × 60W = 240W = 10A if all peaked together — over budget. Fine for music (crest factor), not for simultaneous full-power test tones on all 4 channels. Main fuse 8A slow-blow catches faults, not routine headroom.
