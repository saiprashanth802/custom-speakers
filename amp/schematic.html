2 · Ground path — star topology

Every board's ground returns on its own dedicated wire to a single physical point: the PSU negative terminal. Nothing is daisy-chained. This is the whole reason the band-split helps — if grounds were chained, the class-D switching return current would ride through the DAC's analog reference anyway.

3 · Analog signal ground (rides with the audio)

Separate from the power-star above: each PCM5102's analog ground travels alongside its OUTL/OUTR wires to the amp input's signal ground, ideally twisted-pair per channel. This signal reference and the power-ground star meet only once, at the star point — so switching-current loops and audio-reference loops never overlap.

  • Twisted pair: OUTL + AGND and OUTR + AGND from each DAC to its two amps.
  • Keep I2S clock/data (BCK/WS/DOUT) runs short and away from the 24V amp wiring.
  • Physically separate the high-current 24V harness from all analog/I2S wiring — cross at right angles if they must cross.

4 · Physical layout & mute transistors

As-built floorplan: DACs + ESP32 across the top, the 4 mute transistors (T1–T4) in a row below, and the power distribution on an elevated tier above (its own board, standoffs over this one — keeps the 24V high-current wiring physically off the signal layer). Each amp's 2-pin "mute" jumper header is emulated by one NPN, and each NPN has its own GPIO (GPIO10–13, contiguous, not strapping/USB/PSRAM pins) so channels can be muted individually during bring-up; firmware gangs them for the global mute.

Mute lines (GPIO10–13, one per NPN) 24V (from power tier) 5V to amp mute header

Fail-safe: wire so a floating/low GPIO at boot = muted; firmware un-mutes only after I2S is streaming and the PCM5102 XSMT is high. On shutdown, mute (GPIOs first) before stopping I2S. The 10k pull-down makes "floating = header open"; if the bench test shows shorted = mute, swap it for a 10k pull-up to 3V3 so floating = header shorted = muted.

  • Header pin A vs B: meter the 2-pin header with the amp powered — the pin at ~0 V is B (GND, emitter side); the other is A (control, collector side). Measure each board; don't assume the silkscreen orientation matches across the four.
  • Transistor legs, TO-92 flat face toward you, legs down: BC547 = C · B · E (left→right). 2N3904 / 2N2222A (TO-92) = E · B · C — the reverse. Swapping C and E leaves the NPN in reverse-active mode with tiny gain; it will not pull the header low reliably.
  • Why GPIO10–13: outside the I2S set (4–9), not strapping (0/3/45/46), not native USB (19/20, in use for UAC), not octal-PSRAM (35–37). They are the FSPI (SPI2) pins, which nothing else on this board uses. All four are broken out on the common S3 Mini / Super Mini clones — check the silkscreen once.
  • Firmware: PIN_MUTE = 10 becomes PIN_MUTE[4] = {10, 11, 12, 13} and setAmpsMuted() writes all four. Not yet changed — see WIRING.md.

5b · AC-01A module: rear-tab wiring

Seen from inside the box, so the switch is on the left (the mirror of the front). The module has no internal link between the inlet and the switch; two short jumpers make the chain. The tab layout comes from the seller's photos (Electronic Spices AC-01A, 2026-10-02). Your unit may differ, so the tests below decide, not this drawing.

  1. Inlet tabs (cord out, fuse in): the C14's centre pin beeps to E. One outer pin beeps straight to N. The other outer pin beeps to L′ only while the fuse is in the drawer; pull the fuse and the beep stops. That is how you know which tab is fused.
  2. Switch poles: with the rocker OFF, no two switch tabs beep. With it ON, exactly two pairs beep. Mark one pair A and the other B.
  3. Rows: each pole has one tab in each row. Jumper L′ to one tab of A and N to the tab of B in the same row. If the two rows have different spacing, use the closer pair as IN. Run the other row (A out, B out) to the PSU.
  4. Check before mains (PSU wires not yet on the PSU): with the rocker ON, the front L′ pin should beep to the PSU-L wire end and the front N pin to the PSU-N wire end. The two PSU wire ends must never beep to each other. If they do, a jumper is on the wrong pole. With the rocker OFF, nothing beeps through.
  5. First power-up: the lamp should light only with the rocker ON. If it glows with the rocker OFF, the IN and OUT rows are swapped. That is harmless, because the switch still breaks both lines, but swap the rows so the lamp means "on".
  6. Fuse: replace whatever came in the drawer (it is moulded "use only T10A") with a T3.15 A 5×20. Most of these drawers have a second slot for a spare.

5c · PSU board: WX-DC2416 open frame (Robu 43120)

Top view, drawn to scale from the seller's datasheet (Rev C/D, 2026-10-02). It shows where the switched L/N and earth land, and which parts of the board are live.

  • Earth: put a metal M4 screw through the ⏚ hole beside the AC terminal, with the ring terminal and a star washer. The silkscreen ⏚ and the filter capacitors next to it mark this as the board's earth point. The enclosure is 3D-printed, so the inlet's E runs straight here as one wire.
  • Other three holes: use nylon standoffs and nylon screws. If one of them connects to −V, a metal standoff on an earthed panel would bond −V to earth, which is the hum loop §5 forbids. Meter each hole against −V and against both AC pins before trusting metal hardware.
  • AC terminal: it is unmarked ("AC 220V") and works either way round, because it feeds an EMI filter and then a bridge rectifier. Use bootlace ferrules on stranded wire, not tinned ends: solder creeps under a screw clamp and the joint works loose.
  • Live areas: everything on the AC side of the transformer is at mains potential, including that side's heatsink. The bulk capacitor holds about 325 V DC after unplugging, so wait a few minutes and measure across it before touching. Keep at least 6 mm of air between the primary side and anything conductive. Fit an insulating cover or a vented printed shroud so a dropped screw or loose wire inside the box can't reach it.
  • Heat: at 82 % efficiency the board sheds about 30 W at 140 W out (far less on average with music). The datasheet rates it for −5 to 40 °C ambient with 5 W/°C derating. Vent the box at the board, keep the fins vertical and keep it away from the DAC and ESP32.
  • DC side: on the Rev C block, use both + and both − positions. Don't land the eight star-ground home-runs in the PSU's terminal. Run one short heavy pair to a star bus (a single bolt or bus bar) and terminate them there; it is still one point (§2).
  • First power-up: no load, amps disconnected. Measure 22.8–25.2 V (24 V ±5 %) at the DC block and confirm polarity before connecting anything.
  • C13 vs C14: the box gets the C14 (male pins) and the cord ends in a C13 (female). With the cord out, the box's pins are dead. Never reverse this by putting a female socket on the box.
  • Order is inlet → fuse → switch → PSU, with the fuse on L. F1 protects the inlet, the switch and the internal wiring. The PSU has its own internal fuse.
  • Why T3.15 A slow-blow: 140 W out at the PSU's 82 % efficiency is about 170 W in. With no PFC (power factor about 0.5–0.6), that is roughly 1.2–1.5 A RMS at 230 V. Cold-start inrush is tens of amps for a few milliseconds, so a fast fuse this small blows on switch-on. This is not the 8 A fuse in WIRING.md, which sits on the 24 V DC side.
  • Why DPST: Indian outlets are often wired with L and N swapped. A single-pole switch could then sit in neutral and leave the PSU primary live while "off". DPST breaks both.
  • Find L at the inlet by continuity, not by pin position. Plug the cord into J1 (not the wall). Beep the plug's L pin against the inlet tabs and mark the tab that beeps as L. Check the wall socket's polarity with a tester; the plug-face L/N drawn above is convention only.
  • Earth: the enclosure is 3D-printed, so there is no chassis to earth. Run one continuous green/yellow wire from J1's E tab to the PSU's ⏚ mounting hole (§5c), ring terminal at the PSU end. Bond any metal a finger can touch from outside to that same wire, for example a metal rear plate or screws that pass through to metal inside. Plastic-only screws and the plastic inlet body need nothing.
  • Printed box: print it in PETG, ABS or ASA, not PLA. PLA softens at about 55–60 °C, and the PSU (about 30 W of heat at full load) and the amps sit in the same box. A printed wall is not flame-rated. Give the PSU a printed or polycarbonate shroud, leave vents above and below it, and keep its primary side clear of the walls. Screw the AC-01A's flange through heat-set brass inserts, not self-tapped plastic, so the inlet can't loosen when the cord is pulled. Tie the mains wires down near each terminal so a wire that comes loose can't swing onto the 24 V or logic side.
  • Never bond −V to earth. The USB-C cable already ties DC ground to the PC's earthed chassis. A second bond here creates an earth loop, which hums. Earth on this side is for safety only, and the star point stays at PSU −V (§2).
  • PSU: WX-DC2416 open frame (Robu 43120). Input is universal 85–265 V, so there is no voltage selector. Its earth point is the ⏚-marked M4 mounting hole; mounting, live areas and heat are in §5c.
  • Wire: 0.75–1.0 mm² multi-strand, mains-rated (1100 V house flex is fine). Keep the run short and along the panel, away from the I2S and analog wiring. Use fully insulated spades or heat-shrink on every tab.
  • Before first power (cord unplugged): plug earth pin to the PSU's ⏚ hole should read under 1 Ω. L to ⏚ and N to ⏚ should read open with the switch both on and off. Do the first power-up with the amps disconnected and measure 24 V at the PSU.

Power budget reminder

The WX-DC2416 gives 5.5 A nominal, 6 A peak (rated 140 W). Four amps at nameplate would draw 10 A, but the 8 Ω woofer amps clip at about 25–30 W on 24 V (about 1.4 A each), and tweeters take a small share of music power. Music fits; full-power test tones on all four channels do not. Past 6 A the PSU's overcurrent protection cuts the rail, so the sound drops out and pops instead of sagging. The 8 A main fuse sits above that 6 A, so it can only catch a PSU whose protection has failed. The 3 A branch fuses still matter, because one faulty amp drawing 4 A would not trip the PSU.
